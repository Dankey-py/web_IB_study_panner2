03Some decisions I made

Why I chose these steps

01

Reviewing AI plans before saving

+

An AI reply may look organised while still suggesting too much work for a particular week. I kept the proposal editable so the student can check its tasks, dates and effort before it becomes a saved plan.

The extra step takes time. I think that is a reasonable trade-off because the student is the one who has to carry out the plan.

02

Keeping the task order simple

+

For the suggested task order, I combined the chosen priority with deadline urgency. The rule gives a visible reason for placing one task before another.

The rule also leaves things out. A task may take longer than expected or matter for a reason the planner has not recorded. I present the result as a suggested order that the student can reconsider. This part uses a fixed rule, rather than an AI model.

03

Separating plans from study records

+

A calendar can be full even when the work has not been done. I kept planned tasks and study sessions as separate records so that the overview can distinguish scheduled work from logged activity.

That distinction also changes how the numbers should be read. A study-time total is a record of entered sessions. Understanding still needs to be judged through the work itself.

04The building plan

What is in the project now

Each stage adds a connection the planner needs: an account owns the records, tasks belong to the workspace, and reviewed AI proposals can become saved work.

  1. Accounts and subjects

    Store the student profile and subject information so later tasks have a context.

  2. Planning and study records

    Connect task editing and the calendar, then keep a separate record of completed study sessions.

  3. AI conversation and review

    Give Danky the saved planner context and let the student review proposed changes before applying them.

05What comes next

Learning from other students

I want to understand where the planner’s suggestions do not fit a student’s week, and use those observations to decide what to change.
