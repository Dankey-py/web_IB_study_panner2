03Some decisions I made

Why I chose these steps

01

Reviewing AI plans before saving

+

There is an editing step between the AI answer and the calendar. A generated plan might misunderstand a deadline or suggest too much work. The student should be able to check the dates and change the tasks first.

This makes the process a bit slower. I think that is reasonable because the plan affects what the student will actually do.

02

Keeping the task order simple

+

The suggested task order combines priority with how close the deadline is. This makes it possible to explain why one task appears before another.

However, the score cannot know everything. A task might take longer than expected, or matter for a reason that is not recorded. It is a suggestion rather than a complete schedule, and this part does not use AI.

03

Separating plans from study records

+

A planned task tells the student what to do. A study session records time that was actually spent. I kept them separate so a full calendar would not automatically count as completed study.

The records still depend on what the student enters. They give useful information, but I should not describe them as proof of better learning.

05What I still want to improve

What I want to test next

I would like to ask other students to plan a sample project, then see where they get stuck. For example, do they understand the review step, and which parts of the AI plan do they change?

I also want to check date handling and the review form on smaller screens. I would use those observations to decide which part needs changing first.

These are proposed next steps. I am not presenting them as completed user research.
