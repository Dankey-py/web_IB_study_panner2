02Functions

What it does.

The functions follow a study task from its subject and deadline to a reviewed plan, then to a record of time spent.

01Subjects and tasks

Give a task enough context

A subject such as Physics HL gives a task a context. The subject’s level and target grade stay in the workspace, while each task describes a particular piece of work.

“Plot the results and check uncertainty” gives a clearer starting point than “work on Physics.” I can add a deadline and scheduled time, then update the task’s status as the work progresses.

02Danky — the AI assistant

Where the student’s judgement matters

Danky uses saved subjects, goals and plans to suggest tasks. That context helps, but it cannot include everything about a student’s week. I placed an editable review between the suggestion and the calendar so the student can decide whether the plan makes sense.

03Study time and progress

What the records can tell me

A focus timer

The focus timer records a study session against a subject. A manual form also lets me record work after finishing it.

A record of study activity

The overview brings recorded time and task completion together. I can compare how much attention each subject has received and decide which work to revisit.

Interpreting the numbers

A subject with little recorded time raises a question about attention. The student still has to interpret it: the work may have taken less time, or a session may not have been logged. I use the record to prompt a review of the workload, without treating time as a measure of understanding.
